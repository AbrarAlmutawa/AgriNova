ANSWER:
Evidence:
Deficit irrigation (DI) is an irrigation regime where the water applied is less than the evapotranspiration (ET) requirements of the crop or orchard, resulting in a net extraction of water from the soil reservoir. Regulated deficit irrigation (RDI) refers to applying deficits only at selected stages of a crop's life cycle. Deficit irrigation can be used during drought years when available irrigation supplies are limited. When implementing this, managers adjust the timing, magnitude, and duration of water deficits—often imposing stress during stress-tolerant growth stages—with the goal of maximizing grower profit across current and subsequent seasons.

Reasoning:
This evidence directly defines deficit irrigation and regulated deficit irrigation, explaining how soil water is affected. It explicitly outlines when and how the practice should be used (i.e., during drought conditions with limited water, specifically during stress-tolerant growth stages to maintain profitability).

Recommendation:
Use deficit irrigation by applying less water than full crop evapotranspiration requirements when available irrigation supplies are limited, such as during drought years. When implementing this practice, plan timing and water restrictions around the crop's life cycle—such as using regulated deficit irrigation during stress-tolerant periods—to optimize water use and maximize overall grower profit.
